The Pentagon Wants Access to a Private UFO Archive

AARO filed a sole-source contracting notice to purchase subscription access to the National UFO Historical Records Center’s private collection. The notice is documented. A contract has not been awarded.

By The UFO Times Editorial Desk  ·  September 8, 2026

The Pentagon’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office has filed a federal contracting notice to purchase a commercial data subscription to a private UFO archive in New Mexico. The notice, posted on SAM.gov on July 31, 2026 and first reported by DefenseScoop on September 2, 2026, identifies the National UFO Historical Records Center as the intended sole-source contractor. No contract has been awarded.

The reason AARO wants the archive, and the reason this moment matters, is rooted in a simple problem: the government lost track of its own UAP history.

Decades of records from Air Force investigations, civilian scientific organizations, and individual researchers were never fully centralized. Much of that material ended up in private hands — held by researchers, donated to nonprofits, or quietly transferred when official programs closed. NUFOHRC is, by most accounts, the largest single repository of that privately held material in North America.

■  What You Need to Know

  • What happened: AARO posted a sole-source contracting notice on SAM.gov on July 31, 2026, to purchase subscription access to NUFOHRC’s private UAP records collection. The response deadline was August 17, 2026. The notice was first reported by DefenseScoop on September 2, 2026.
  • The collection: NUFOHRC, based in Rio Rancho, New Mexico, holds the historical files of NICAP, CUFOS, and the personal archive of Dr. J. Allen Hynek, the Air Force’s own scientific advisor on Project Blue Book. The organization is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded by David Marler.
  • Why pre-1990: The contracting notice specifically targets materials that pre-date 1990 — the period not covered by modern digital records systems and the focus of Congress’s mandate for AARO to review U.S. government UAP involvement back to 1945.
  • Contract structure: One base year plus four one-year option periods — up to five years of subscription access to NUFOHRC’s data, metadata, and analytical products.
  • Not established: The contract has not been awarded. The value has not been disclosed. NUFOHRC did not respond to press inquiries before publication.

An anonymous source described to DefenseScoop as a “War Department official” said NUFOHRC “possesses the world’s largest private collection of UAP-related historical records, including hundreds of thousands of unique case files that exist exclusively within its archives.” That characterization reflects the government’s apparent assessment, not an independently verified count.

What NUFOHRC Actually Holds

Project Blue Book — the Air Force’s primary UFO investigation program, which ran from 1952 to 1969 — produced thousands of case files now held at the National Archives. But the records that most interested researchers were never fully in government hands. J. Allen Hynek, the astronomer who served as the Air Force’s scientific consultant throughout the program and later founded the Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS), maintained his own files. Those files eventually passed to CUFOS and then to Marler, who became their official archivist.

NUFOHRC also holds the complete historical case files of the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP), one of the most credible civilian UFO research organizations of the Cold War era, and the files of the Aerial Phenomena Research Organization (APRO). These collections cover field investigations, witness testimony, photographs, physical evidence, and correspondence that was never submitted to the government. Some of it may document incidents that appeared in official reports only in truncated form.

That gap — between what the government collected and what private researchers documented — is what AARO now wants to close.

The Congressional Mandate Behind the Procurement

The 2023 National Defense Authorization Act directed AARO to compile a comprehensive historical record report covering all U.S. government UAP-related intelligence, military engagements, and related programs from 1945 onward. The 2024 NDAA created the UAP Records Collection at the National Archives — Record Group 615 — and directed every federal agency to identify, digitize, and transfer all of its UAP records to NARA on a rolling basis.

Eight agencies have begun doing so: the FAA, the Nuclear Regulatory Commission, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, the Office of the Secretary of Defense, the NSA, the State Department, the FBI, and the Department of the Air Force. NARA says the collection will continue to grow as additional transfers arrive.

The problem is that those government transfers only cover what the government held. The civilian record — the investigations conducted outside official channels, by researchers who sometimes had better access to witnesses than Air Force investigators did — has no government equivalent. NUFOHRC is, in the government’s own apparent assessment, the closest thing to a complete civilian archive.

“Market research conducted to date indicates that no other organization possesses the historical depth, volume of unique records, and specialized analytical expertise to meet the government’s minimum needs.”

The practical question is not whether the archive is real — it is. The question is what AARO expects to find. Pre-1990 civilian UFO investigations documented many cases that the government did not adequately explain at the time. Whether those cases contain anything the government does not already possess, or anything that would change the current scientific and intelligence assessment of UAP, is unknown. That is, presumably, what the subscription is intended to determine.

What this procurement does not mean: it does not imply that the government believes NUFOHRC holds evidence of alien technology. It implies that the government believes it may have incomplete records and that a private organization has data worth examining. That is a statement about archival gaps, not about extraterrestrial contact.
