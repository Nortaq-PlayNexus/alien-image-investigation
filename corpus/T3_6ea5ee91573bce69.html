Pentagon releases sixth batch of UFO files

Tatiana Carter

Updated: Sep 18, 2026 / 02:59 PM CDT

(NewsNation) — The Pentagon released another wave of documents, videos and images Friday morning, marking the sixth release under the government’s effort to make records about UFOs public.

The latest release adds to hundreds of files made public since May.

Pentagon issues waiver to allow personnel to come forward on UAPs

Several files are from the 1950s, related to the Air Force’s Project Blue Book, which was tasked with investigating UFO sightings.

One document details a report of an unidentified object from the sea off Okinawa in November 1950. An October 1952 memo called a request to search for and attempt to recover the object impractical.

A 1953 document, which has previously been available to the public, described abrupt changes and direction by objects, calculating acceleration as high as 965 times gravity.

The release also includes a highly anticipated audio recording from a 1952 Project Blue Book briefing that included information about the Air Force’s use of Battelle Memorial Institute as a contractor.

Rep. Eric Burlison, R-Mo., pushed the MIT Lincoln Laboratory to release the recording, identifying the tape by name and asking it to be digitized and transferred to the National Archives.

Ross Coulthart Q&A: Did nonhuman intelligence really warn astronauts off the moon? | Reality Check

The release is part of the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters, or PURSUE, which began releasing records in May 2026. Previous batches were released May 8, May 22, June 12, July 10 and Aug. 7. The records have come from multiple federal agencies, including the Pentagon, FBI, CIA, State Department and Executive Office of the President.

The files are reviewed as part of the government’s UFO investigation efforts. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, or AARO, has said small metallic orbs are among the most commonly reported types of UAP. AARO has also said it has found no verifiable evidence that any investigated incident involved extraterrestrial technology, while many cases remain unresolved.
